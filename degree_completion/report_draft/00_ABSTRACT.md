# Abstract

Reddit's Change My View (CMV) subreddit offers an observable, comment-level signal of persuasive success: the delta (∆) award. This study investigates whether delta-award status can be predicted from textual features, and whether explicitly defined rhetorical strategies add predictive information beyond comment length. Using a dataset of 3,971 CMV comments (641 delta-awarded, 16.1%) with thread-grouped train/test splits, an engineered-feature pipeline combining sentiment, tone, rhetorical-appeal, readability, and evidence-marker measures reached held-out PR-AUC 0.3848, below a single-feature word-count baseline (0.5256) and a TF-IDF bag-of-words model (0.5119) — revealing comment length as the dominant predictive signal and motivating the length-controlled analyses that follow. Eight rhetorical strategies (including refutation, justification, and concession), scored on a 1-5 scale by an LLM-based rubric adapted from Labruna et al. (2026), were then added to the engineered baseline on a 2,753-comment scored subset, raising PR-AUC from 0.4434 to 0.5098 (+0.0664), a gain that retains 84% of its value under strict length matching, indicating the gain is not purely a length proxy. Individual-strategy importance depends on the measure used: refutation has the highest SHAP-based model attribution, while justification has the largest univariate rank-biserial effect size. Applying the same approach to Winning Arguments, an independently constructed persuasion corpus, replicates the structural finding that strategy scores add value beyond length, but the strategy-level pattern reverses: justification, not refutation, dominates by both measures. The study's contribution is primarily empirical: a documented account of length's predictive dominance, a length-controlled assessment of rhetorical-strategy value, and a cross-corpus evaluation distinguishing what generalizes from what does not.
